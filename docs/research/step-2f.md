## What good mock analysis does (both guides agree)
1. Note the score, attempts, right, wrong, accuracy and time **before** reading solutions.
2. Look at every question, not only wrong ones. Cracku's seven groups:
   | Group | Action |
   |---|---|
   | Right and quick | keep |
   | Right but slow | practise speed |
   | Wrong: concept gap | revise |
   | Wrong: careless | check better |
   | Wrong: guessed | choose better what to attempt |
   | Skipped, but doable | why was it skipped? marks left on the table |
   | Skipped and hard | a good skip, no action |
3. Re-solve the skipped questions **untimed** (10–15 minutes) before reading solutions, to prove whether they were doable.
4. Split time: on right answers, on wrong answers, on questions given up. Time on given-up questions should be as low as possible.
5. Set a target (attempts) before the mock; compare after.
6. Act on the **two or three mistakes that cost the most marks** before the next mock.
7. Patterns show only across **five to ten mocks**; keep a running sheet.
8. Do not take the next mock before analysing the last.

Cause lists in the guides: concept, application, calculation, reading, selection (choosing what to attempt), time, guessing, overconfidence, panic, silly slip.

## CAT VARC pattern (2024 and 2025, all three slots)
- 24 questions: 4 RC passages × 4 questions, then 8 VA: 2 para summary, 2 para completion (sentence insertion), 2 odd sentence out, 2 para jumbles.
- TITA (no negative marks): odd sentence out and para jumbles, 4 questions. All others MCQ: +3, −1.
- IMS: about 10 to 12 RC questions could be done with high accuracy in 2025; RCs were the tough part.

## Weekly reflection
- A three-year study had students write weekly reflections on planning, monitoring and evaluating their work; the reflecting cohort gained more on a metacognition inventory (p = 0.037). Exam "wrappers" (what went wrong, what to change) are used for the same reason. Small, quasi-experimental studies: supports a short weekly prompt, not a big feature.

## Weekly review (2f-2), 21 Oct 2026
- Exam wrappers, also used on weekly quizzes, ask four things in order: how did you prepare, how did it go against what you expected, what did you miss and why, what will you do differently. A 2025 review of 30 studies found gains in metacognition, self-regulation and achievement, strongest when paired with training; only five were randomised trials, mostly STEM. Sources: https://serc.carleton.edu/58198 , https://teaching.fsu.edu/?p=214 , https://pressbooks.uiowa.edu/hic-toolkit/?p=67 , https://aalhe.scholasticahq.com/article/129036.pdf
- So the review follows that order: the week in numbers (preparation), improved and slipped (how it went), the trap of the week and redo (what was missed), then one focus and an optional "what will you do differently?" line (the change). Goal-setting is one focus, not a list.
- Change from the plan: the focus appears on Today as a row "Focus of the week" until it is done three times that week, instead of changing the planner engine. Same effect, no risk to the timetable.
