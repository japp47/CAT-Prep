## Findings
1. **Neon's free compute stops when the month's hours run out.** 100 CU-hours a month is about 400 hours at the smallest size (0.25 CU); once used, compute is suspended until the next month. It sleeps after 5 minutes idle and wakes in a few hundred milliseconds. Students across India study from early morning to midnight, so with even a few dozen active students the database is awake most of 16–18 hours a day (about 500 hours a month): sync would stop for the last days of the month. Storage is 0.5 or 1 GB (Neon's own pages disagree). Region nearest India: Singapore.
2. **Supabase's free Postgres has no hour cap.** It is always on while used, pauses only after 7 days with no activity (not a problem once students use it), 500 MB per project, two projects, and has a Mumbai region. We would use it as plain Postgres through `pgx` (none of its auth or APIs), so moving between Neon and Supabase is a change of `DATABASE_URL`. Catch: the direct connection is IPv6; from Cloud Run use the session pooler (IPv4, keeps prepared statements working). Figures are from third-party pages; check the official page.
3. **Cloud Run** still gives 2M requests and 180K vCPU-seconds a month free (request-based billing), counted per billing account, so it needs a card. Mumbai (`asia-south1`) exists. Render free needs no card but takes about a minute to wake and has 750 hours a month. Koyeb no longer has a free web service.
4. **Cloudflare now points new projects to Workers with static assets.** Pages keeps working and gets fixes; there is no shutdown date. Pages Functions are billed like Workers. No reason to move now; the proxy can be a Pages Function today and move with the site later.
5. **Turnstile is free with no challenge limit** (20 widgets). One widget for "turn on sync" and later sign-up.
6. **Don't order sync by device clocks.** Replicache's docs say timestamps cannot be relied on and that ordering comes from server counters; pushes are checked against the last version the server recorded. Our plan used the device's `updatedAt` for "newest wins": a phone with its clock a day ahead would win every conflict for a day.
7. **Safari deletes a site's storage after 7 days of Safari use without a visit.** Home-screen web apps have their own counter that resets when they are used (reports differ on whether opening is enough). So an iPhone student who studies in Safari, takes a week off and comes back can find their progress gone. `navigator.storage.persist()` asks the browser to keep data (granted more readily to installed apps). This is a reason for sync, and a reason to nudge iPhone students to add 99ile to the home screen, both of which help today.
8. **Anonymous accounts work when recovery is honest.** Mullvad gives only an account number, says recovery is not guaranteed, and keeps no email. Students understand "write this down" when the screen says plainly what happens if they lose it.
9. **DPDP Rules 2025** were notified on 13 Nov 2025; most duties (notice, consent, rights, security, breach reporting) apply from 13 May 2027. Anonymous sync holds no name or email, but logs with IP addresses are personal data: keep them short-lived. Accounts (email) need a privacy notice and export and delete before May 2027; we plan them in step 3 anyway.
10. **Go 1.23 is out of support** (a release is supported until two newer ones exist; 1.26 and 1.27 are current; the owner's Mac has 1.27.3). `go.mod` says 1.23.

## Addendum: QR code to link devices (21 Oct 2026, owner asked for it)
Sources: iOS QR links open outside the Home Screen app: https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/122043 ; BarcodeDetector support (iOS Safari: disabled by default through 26.6, Chrome Android: yes): https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_barcodedetector ; WhatsApp's linking (scan from inside the app, confirm, device list): https://meetergo.com/en/magazine/whatsapp-web-qr-code ; libraries on npm: `uqr` 0.1.3 (MIT, QR to SVG, small), `jsqr` 1.4.0 (Apache-2.0, pure JS decoder).

11. **The phone's camera app is the wrong scanner.** On iPhone it opens the link in Safari (or a web view without Safari's data), and a Home Screen app keeps its storage apart from Safari. So a QR scanned with the camera can link the wrong copy of 99ile. WhatsApp scans only from inside its own app for the same kind of reason. **Decision:** 99ile has its own "Scan the code" screen using the camera; the QR still holds a full link so Android's camera and a laptop also work.
12. **iPhone Safari cannot decode QR codes for a web page by itself** (`BarcodeDetector` is off by default through iOS 26.6). Use `BarcodeDetector` where it exists (Chrome on Android), else the `jsqr` decoder, loaded only when the scanner opens.
13. **One direction is enough.** The device that already syncs shows the QR and the code; the new device scans it, or types the 8 characters (a laptop has a keyboard, a phone has a camera). A WhatsApp-style reverse flow (new laptop shows a QR, phone approves) needs a pending-request table and polling, and adds nothing a typed code on a laptop does not already do. Not built.
14. **Our own headers block the camera:** `public/_headers` sends `Permissions-Policy: camera=()`. It must become `camera=(self)` in B4.
15. **Phishing:** someone could show a student their own QR. Low value here (study progress), but the new device is named in the device list on every linked device, and the scan screen says "Only scan a code shown on your own device."
16. **A Home Screen app on iPhone does not share storage with Safari, by Apple's design** (https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=181849). It opens empty. So "add 99ile to your Home Screen" must also say how to bring progress across: download a backup in Safari, restore it in the Home Screen app (until sync ships), then link it with the QR (after B4). The Install card in Settings said only "Add to Home Screen": fixed in S5. The sync recovery and link flows also have to work inside the Home Screen app, which is another reason the QR scanner lives in 99ile.
17. **Chrome, Edge and Firefox on iPhone can add a site to the Home Screen** since iOS 16.4 (Chrome: Share, to the right of the address bar → Add to Home Screen; https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/15085120?co=GENIE.Platform%3DiOS). Each iPhone browser keeps its own copy of a site's data, so the install steps must never send a Chrome student to Safari. Settings now gives the steps for the browser in use. Whether Safari's 7-day clearing also applies inside Chrome is unclear (WebKit's tracking prevention is on in every iPhone browser since iOS 14, but the 7-day rule is described in terms of Safari use), so the 7-day note stays Safari-only; sync (B5) covers both.
18. **Chrome on iPhone does not always offer Add to Home Screen** (owner's test, 21 Oct 2026, on what looks like a cloud test phone). Reasons found: the action can be hidden by the user (fix: Edit Actions), some Chrome and iOS versions lack it (sources put the change between iOS 16.4 and 17.2), and Chrome's web-share sheet never has it, only the share icon in its address bar (https://github.com/fertheoz/fxvps.ai/pull/80 ; https://intercom.help/progressier/en/articles/7948220-i-can-t-find-the-add-to-home-screen-button-on-my-iphone). Test phones and emulators may not show it at all. A page cannot add the action. The guide and Settings now say: Edit Actions first; if still missing, move to Safari with a backup (each browser keeps its own data).
