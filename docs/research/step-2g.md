## Findings
1. **Confidence matters.** Errors made with high confidence are corrected well right after feedback (86% on an immediate retest), but a week later only 56% stayed corrected and confident errors tended to come back. One round of feedback is not enough; spaced retesting is. Low-confidence right answers ("lucky guesses") should surprise the student and are worth a retest. Small study (50 students); treat as direction, not a number.
2. **Self-diagnosis is unreliable.** Readers, especially weaker ones, misjudge their own comprehension ("metacognitive illusions"). A cause the student picks is a useful prompt but weak evidence.
3. **One thing at a time.** Learning systems that recommend one item at a time, with "skip for another", avoid choice overload; overload makes people confused and less satisfied. Two to four options is practitioner advice, not a tested threshold.
4. **Small samples are noisy** (our own arithmetic): accuracy on 12 questions at 60% has a standard error of about 14 points; on 8 questions about 17. A 10-point change on 8 questions is noise.

## What changes in the plan
| Finding | Change |
|---|---|
| Confident errors come back after a week | Keep every wrong answer in the spaced redo queue (already so). "Not sure" (F45) marks low confidence: a right answer marked "not sure" also goes to the redo queue once |
| Lucky guesses deserve a retest | Report card shows "right but not sure" by type |
| Self-diagnosis is unreliable | Patterns (F42) are built first from the trap labels in the content (objective), then from causes the student picked; a picked cause is pre-filled only from data (very fast answer: careless; "not sure": guessed) and is always editable |
| One recommendation at a time | Next step (F41) shows one action, with "Something else" to see the next rule's suggestion, never a list |
| Small samples are noisy | "Fixed" (F43) needs 12 or more questions after and a rise of 15 points or more; under 12 questions it says "Too early to tell" with the count. Patterns need 4 errors in a pair and 6 questions of the type (unchanged) |
